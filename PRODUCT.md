# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Clients and employers evaluating whether to hire or collaborate. They land on the portfolio to quickly assess technical skill, design sensibility, and professional identity. They may be browsing on desktop or mobile, often comparing multiple candidates side by side.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio that showcases the owner's full-stack and creative capabilities — not just what was built, but how it was built. The site itself is the strongest credential. Success means the visitor leaves thinking "this person is exceptional" and reaches out.

## Brand Personality

Bold, technical, futuristic. Confident without arrogance. The tone is direct and capable — the work speaks, the site amplifies.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS landing pages with hero-metric templates (big number, small label, gradient accent)
- Overused AI-design tells: cream/sand beige backgrounds, gradient text, side-stripe borders, uppercase "ABOUT" / "PROJECTS" / "SKILLS" eyebrows above every section
- Cookie-cutter portfolio templates that look like every other developer's site
- Dull, flat, muted "safe" corporate design

## Design Principles

1. **The medium is the message.** The portfolio itself must demonstrate the skill it advertises. Every design decision is a proof point.
2. **Stand out, don't fit in.** The goal is to be remembered. Safe choices lose.
3. **Dark and dimensional.** The dark palette, mesh gradients, 3D elements, and motion create a immersive, memorable atmosphere — not a flat document.
4. **Clarity under spectacle.** Motion and effects must not obscure the work. Content hierarchy stays readable and fast.
5. **Every pixel intentional.** Nothing is default. Spacing, color, typography, and interaction are all deliberate choices.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA as a baseline. Dark theme with sufficient contrast on all text. Reduced motion respected. The 3D and animated elements degrade gracefully — they enhance but are never the only way to access content.
